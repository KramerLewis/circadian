## Details

### More widgets: CarPlay
iOS 26 shows iPhone widgets in CarPlay (no CarPlay app entitlement needed; full CarPlay
apps are limited to categories like navigation and audio). Make sure the Sun Ring small
widget is offered there and reads at a glance: the ring has no numbers, which suits
driving; the medium widget's text is less glanceable. Alarm Live Activities may also
appear in CarPlay on iOS 26. Look up the current WidgetKit CarPlay rules when building
this; CI can't capture CarPlay (Apple's CarPlay Simulator needs a Mac), so verify in
the car via TestFlight.

### Sun over time
See how the sun's events shift over a chosen span (a month, a season, a year):
- A chart with dates along one axis and time of day along the other: sunrise, sunset,
  solar noon, and the civil, nautical and astronomical twilights as lines, with
  daylight and the twilight bands shaded between them (the familiar "sun graph").
  DST jumps show as steps in clock time; a toggle could plot solar time instead.
- Day length on its own, with the change per day ("+2m 31s since yesterday").
- Special days and eclipses on the same timeline: solstices and equinoxes (where the
  curves turn), full, super and blue moons, new moons, and eclipses visible from the
  place, as markers you can tap for the banner text. Moonrise/moonset could be an
  optional layer.
- Pick a day on the chart to jump the dial to it; Watch and web versions later (the
  web app already has all the math).
- All on device from SunCore: a year is 365 solar days plus one SkyCalendar and
  eclipse search, fast enough to compute when opened. Swift Charts draws it.
- Needs a design pass: where it lives (a tab or from the event list), default span,
  and how polar days (no sunrise or sunset) look on the chart.

### Seasonal and live NASA Earth imagery
The 3D view uses NASA's Blue Marble for June 2004 and the Black Marble 2016 night lights
(`shared/assets/nasa/`). Next steps:
- **Seasons**: Blue Marble Next Generation comes in 12 monthly versions (snow cover,
  sea ice and vegetation change through the year). Use the month of the date being
  shown, blending between neighboring months, so scrubbing into winter whitens the north.
  Twelve 2048 x 1024 JPEGs are about 4 MB, fine for the web (loaded on demand) and for
  an app bundle.
- **Started**: `.github/workflows/nasa-assets.yml` fetches what `shared/tools/nasa_sources.json` lists
  and converts it (the Deep Star Map EXR so far). Add the Blue Marble months there.
- **Automatic updates**: a scheduled GitHub Actions job (CI runners can reach NASA,
  which this cloud session can't) fetches NASA imagery, resizes it with
  `shared/tools/make_earth_textures.py`, and commits or publishes it with the web app:
  - Blue Marble months: fetched once (they don't change).
  - Night lights: NASA's Black Marble products are updated yearly; re-fetch when a new
    composite appears.
  - Near real time: NASA GIBS serves daily true-color mosaics (VIIRS / MODIS, with that
    day's clouds) and nightly lights as tiles. That's a step toward Weather (13): the
    globe as it looked yesterday.
- **Sun**: NASA SDO HMI intensitygrams, refreshed monthly for the web by the NASA assets
  workflow; the iPhone app bundles a copy that changes only with regular releases (no
  release just for images). Later: the web app loads SDO's latest image live (SDO
  updates every 15 minutes), and the iPhone app could download and cache it itself
  (no release needed; note the request to NASA in the privacy policy).
- Record the source, date and URL of each image next to it, and keep the credits in the
  README and the app's about screen current.

### Wadokei animal art
Done on the web: twelve line drawings (Recraft, in `shared/assets/zodiac/`, all facing
left), baked to masks in `web/public/zodiac/` by `web/scripts/zodiac-masks.mjs` and lit in

### Historical clocks
Time displays from older systems live in their own group (Display → Time Display →
Historical Clocks; tapping the dial cycles within the group), so the everyday cycle stays
short. Roman hours are done (12 from sunrise to sunset, 4 night watches, on web, iPhone
and Watch); the wadokei is in the group too. Candidates, all built like the wadokei and
Roman hours (split day and night into stretching parts):
- **Canonical hours**: Lauds, Prime, Terce, Sext, None, Vespers, Compline on the Roman
  hours (noon comes from None, the ninth hour).
- **Hindu prahar** (four watches of day, four of night) with **muhurta** (30 a day; the
  15 of daylight stretch) and **ghati** (60 a day).
- **Egyptian hours**: 12 of night by the rising decan stars (needs star data and
  research), 12 of day.
- **Chinese shichen**: 12 equal double hours with the same zodiac animals (the wadokei art
  carries over), and ke (1/100 day). Not sun-relative, so lower priority.
Considered and skipped: Islamic prayer times and Jewish zmanim (several competing
calculation methods; dedicated apps do them well and people rely on them), French decimal
time (not sun-based).

### Relative-time clock
12-hour and 24-hour are clock times; "relative" deserves its own clock rather than a line
of text. Ideas for the design pass:
- **Four quadrants** of the solar day, each named by a letter, with the time since its
  start: for example M (solar midnight to sunrise), R (sunrise to solar noon), N (solar
  noon to sunset), S (sunset to solar midnight), shown as "N 1:48" for 1h 48m after
  solar noon. The quadrants are unequal (daylight is long in summer), which is the point:
  the reading follows the sun, not the clock.
- Or count toward the next anchor ("S −0:42" = 42 minutes before sunset), or show a
  fraction of the quadrant (like the ancient unequal "temporal hours", six per quadrant).
- **Lunar relative time** (optional): the same with moonrise, moon highest, moonset and
  moon lowest as the anchors, in its own letters or style, perhaps as a second smaller
  readout or a toggle.
- Polar days: when there's no sunrise or sunset, fall back to two quadrants (noon and
  midnight) or to the 24-hour solar time.
- Where it appears: the dial's center readout, widgets, complications and alarms.
- Decide letters, formats, and whether the dial's ring shows the quadrant boundaries.

### Saved places
A list of places (home, family, a trip) to view the dial, 3D view, events and special
days for, alongside the current location. The model already has a place; this makes it
a list (SwiftData, sync-ready like the rest), with a switcher in the title bar and a
widget setting to pick which place a widget shows. Each place keeps its own time zone
(needed for anywhere other than here; the web app already stores one). Alarms stay tied
to where you are, unless an alarm is explicitly pinned to a saved place.

### Effective sunrise and sunset from terrain
The astronomical sunrise assumes a flat horizon. In a valley or behind a ridge the sun
appears later and disappears earlier; on a west-facing slope evening light lasts longer.
- Build a horizon profile for the place: for each compass direction, the highest angle
  of the terrain around it out to ~50 km, by marching along the ground in an elevation
  model (Copernicus GLO-30 or SRTM, 30 m; tiles could come from an API like
  Open-Meteo's elevation endpoint, or be bundled per region). Include Earth's curvature
  and refraction. Compute once per place and cache.
- Effective events: when the sun's disc clears and drops below that profile, and how
  long a spot is in direct sun each day ("direct sun 7:52 AM-4:18 PM"). Show both on
  the dial (the effective arc inside the astronomical one) and in the event list.
- Simulated light: how bright and warm the light is through the day, from the sun's
  altitude, the terrain shadow, and (with weather) cloud cover. Could also drive alarms
  ("when the sun clears the ridge").
- With weather (13): mark when cloud is forecast to hide the sun at those times.
- Decisions: elevation source (API vs bundled), how far to look, and whether to use
  the phone's exact location (needed here: terrain changes over a few hundred meters,
  so reduced accuracy isn't enough; ask for precise location just for this feature).

### View as a friend's place, and friends on the 3D view
Apple doesn't let third-party apps read Find My locations (there is no public API for
friends' locations), so this can't come from Find My directly. Options:
- **Manual**: add a friend's city as a saved place (8c) and "view as" it. Works now.
- **In-app sharing**: friends who use SunClock share their approximate location with
  each other through the sync backend (P2), opt-in, revocable, and coarse (city level).
  Then a toggle shows them as pins on the 3D Earth; tap one to view their clock,
  daylight and special days ("it's 2h after sunset for Sam").
- Contacts can give an address for a friend, which is another manual route.
- Privacy first: off by default, no precise locations, and clear in the privacy policy.

### Weather
Real weather over the 3D Earth and on the dial:
- **Now**: satellite cloud imagery mapped onto the globe (NASA GIBS serves global
  imagery tiles freely; geostationary satellites such as GOES and Himawari update every
  10-15 minutes), with clouds lifted to their heights from cloud-top products, so the
  terminator and moonlight fall across real cloud.
- **Forecast**: generate the sky from forecast data instead (cloud cover and heights by
  layer, precipitation, wind; e.g. NOAA GFS or Open-Meteo), drawn as simulated clouds,
  and waves on the sea from wave forecasts (NOAA WAVEWATCH III: height, period,
  direction).
- Ties in with effective sunrise (11): "sunrise hidden by low cloud tomorrow".
- Big and network-dependent: needs data sources and licensing checked, likely a backend
  to cache and tile data, and a rendering approach (RealityKit on iPhone, WebGL on the
  web). A first step could be just the current satellite cloud layer on the 3D Earth.

### Tides in the center
Show the tide for the current location inside both rings (rising/falling, next
high/low, height curve for the day).
- **Not computable from location alone.** Tide prediction needs per-station harmonic
  constants (NOAA CO-OPS for the US; other agencies elsewhere, often licensed). Options:
  bundle constants for a set of stations and predict on-device (keeps "no network"),
  or fetch from an API (breaks it, and coverage/licensing varies by country).
- Inland locations need a "nearest station" rule or should just hide the tide.
- Decision needed before building.

### Eclipses
Show upcoming solar and lunar eclipses correctly for the location: type (partial,
annular, total; penumbral/partial/total lunar), local contact times, and the visible
magnitude, and render them on the dial.
- Built: computed on device from the Meeus ch. 47 moon and the NOAA sun (shadow
  geometry for lunar eclipses, topocentric disc overlap for solar), so no bundled
  data. Contact times agree with PyEphem to about a minute. Shown as special days
  (blood moon tints the moon red; the moon's disc crosses the sun on the dial), with a
  heads-up, the next eclipse in the moon list, widgets and the Watch.

### Special event days
Distinct UI treatments on notable days: solstices, equinoxes, full/new moons,
supermoons, eclipses, maybe cross-quarter days. Needs a design pass: what each looks
like and how loud it is.

### Cat feeder on solar time
Feed the cats relative to solar events (e.g. "at civil dawn", "1 hr before sunset"),
the same rules the alarms use.

**Open question: which feeder?** The approach depends on the hardware:
- **Hackable / DIY (ESP32, ESPHome, Arduino):** run the solar math on the device
  itself, ported to C++ and tested against `shared/fixtures`. Most reliable: no
  phone, no cloud, works through Wi-Fi outages. The app could push the rules to it
  over the local network (or via the sync backend later).
- **Smart plug / HomeKit-controlled feeder:** schedule from the phone with HomeKit
  automations; sunrise/sunset are built in, but twilight, solar noon and offsets
  aren't, and HomeKit can't use our calculations directly.
- **Vendor cloud feeder (Petlibro, PetSafe, etc.):** only if it has a usable API;
  the backend would push daily feed times. Most fragile.
- Whichever it is: expose an App Intent ("next time of <event> + offset") so
  Shortcuts and automations can use SunClock's times too.

### Native macOS app + widget
For now, the iPhone's Sun Ring widget can be shown on the Mac desktop (System Settings
› Desktop & Dock › Widgets › Use iPhone widgets); it needs the iPhone signed in to the
same Apple ID and nearby or on the same network. A native version removes that:
- New macOS app target: a small window with the dial, readouts and today's events.
  Alarms stay iPhone-only (no AlarmKit on macOS).
- macOS widget extension reusing `Shared/` (ring renderer, widget content) and SunCore,
  which already builds for macOS.
- Make `Shared/` cross-platform: `DialPalette` and the system colors use UIKit
  (`UIColor`), so they need `NSColor` equivalents.
- Its own location permission (CoreLocation works on macOS) plus the manual fallback;
  later, sync from the backend.
- Distribution via the same developer account: TestFlight for Mac / Mac App Store, or
  a notarized download. CI can build it on the macOS runner.

### Stage 5: Apple Watch (design agreed)
Third-party apps can't make watch faces, so SunClock ships complications for Apple's
faces, a Smart Stack card and a Watch app. Mockups reviewed Oct 22.
- **Complications** (WidgetKit accessory families, reusing `Shared/` renderers):
  - Circular: sun ring with the glowing sun and the real moon in the center;
    full color where the face allows, tinted otherwise.
  - Corner: curved daylight-progress gauge with "SUNSET 4:51"; at night, time to sunrise.
  - Rectangular (also the Smart Stack card): ring + next sunrise/sunset + live countdown
    + moon phase.
  - Inline: "1h 51m until sunset" (or solar time; to confirm).
- **Watch app:** full-screen dial (sun + moon rings) with the same center readout and
  time-display setting as the phone (tap to cycle); swipe for events and the moon;
  dimmed always-on mode updating once a minute.
  - Open: Digital Crown scrubbing through time (snaps back to now) vs. no scrubbing.
- **Data:** the phone's current place and settings via WatchConnectivity; the Watch's
  own location as a fallback. SunCore runs on watchOS (already declared in Package.swift).
- **Alarms:** stay on the iPhone (AlarmKit); verify on a real Watch that they mirror.
- **Testing:** no Apple Watch on hand, so CI watchOS simulator screenshots until a
  device test is possible.
